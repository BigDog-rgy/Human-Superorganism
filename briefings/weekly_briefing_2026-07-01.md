## Phase Sequence Pulse

**DPS-01: Executive Power Consolidation** [6/10] — *CA-006, CA-030, CA-049*  
The past week was relatively quiet on *new* formal moves to consolidate presidential control, but it occurred in the shadow of an extraordinary structural shift: the Supreme Court’s recent decisions in **Trump v. Cook** and **Trump v. Slaughter**, which effectively eliminated the independence of nearly all federal agencies except the Federal Reserve and “gave carte blanche to the Trump Administration” to continue reshaping the bureaucracy.[1] This jurisprudential change, combined with Trump’s earlier “Ensuring Accountability for All Agencies” directive that all agencies “must be supervised and controlled” by the president and route significant regulatory actions through the White House’s OIRA, means that even routine White House personnel and management decisions this week operate within a much more centralized, unitary-executive framework.[2][3] Public trackers show no major new executive orders in the past seven days specifically targeting bureaucracy structure or a fresh reduction-in-force, but the cumulative count of 269 executive orders in the second term and ongoing implementation of prior OMB and DOGE-style efficiency mandates indicate that agencies are still under strong pressure to align operations with White House control.[3][8]

**DPS-02: AI Dominance Race** [7/10] — *CA-005, CA-129, CA-099, CA-070, CA-011*  
The biggest development in the past week was the **White House’s new AI executive order**, which moves federal policy toward tighter security oversight of frontier models by creating a classified benchmarking process and a voluntary pre-release access framework for covered models[7]. That is a structural shift because it brings national-security agencies more directly into model evaluation, early access, and trusted-partner selection without imposing a formal licensing regime[7]. The broader context remains an intensifying global race in which frontier capabilities, enterprise adoption, and compute infrastructure are all becoming more central to competitive advantage[1][3][4]. The available results for this specific week do not show comparably large new model launches, funding rounds, or compute-deal announcements, so the week appears driven more by policy than by commercial product momentum[1][7][10].
