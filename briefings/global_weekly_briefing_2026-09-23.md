The most significant development was an eight-hour US-China negotiation in New York that produced agreement to establish a formal **bilateral AI dialogue**, including a proposed mechanism for notifying each other about AI-related national-security incidents.[1][6] The talks also addressed extending the existing trade truce, reducing selected tariffs, Chinese rare-earth and critical-mineral exports, and US technology restrictions, indicating limited crisis management without resolving the underlying economic rivalry.[2][5] The meetings were explicitly aimed at preparing President Trump’s September 24 meeting with Xi Jinping, making high-level stabilization the week’s central diplomatic signal.[1][5] Overall, the week showed intensified engagement and tactical de-escalation, but continued structural competition over advanced technology, supply chains, Taiwan, and trade leverage.[5][7]

Allied air cooperation nevertheless continued to deepen, with French and South Korean forces conducting high-end airpower drills that improved coalition interoperability and operational reach in the Indo-Pacific.[15] Overall, the week showed continued architecture consolidation rather than a major new institutional breakthrough.

**PS-04: Semiconductor and Compute Chokepoint Control** [7/10]

**PS-05: Frontier AI Power Struggle** [8/10] — *CA-043, CA-103, CA-131*

**PS-08: Sanctions, Countersanctions, and Geoeconomic Decoupling** [8/10] — *CA-246, CA-215, CA-014*

**PS-09: Information Ecosystem and Narrative Control** [6/10]  
The week’s clearest structural development was the European Union’s proposed **EU Kids Act**, which would bar under-13s from social media, place 13–14-year-olds in parent-supervised “mini accounts,” require age verification, and force platforms to remove or limit addictive design features.[13][14] The proposal would also extend safeguards to video platforms, online games, app stores, and AI chatbots, with potential penalties of up to 6% of global turnover—signaling a broader shift from content moderation toward regulating access, recommendation systems, and platform design.[7][12] California’s newly enacted restrictions on behaviorally addictive features for under-16s reinforce this transatlantic regulatory momentum, although they predate the seven-day window and are implementation rather than announcement news.[5] No comparably significant ownership change, major influence-operation takedown, or state-media restructuring was evident in the available reporting for this period.

**PS-11: Middle-Power and Global South Realignment** [8/10] — *CA-208, CA-009, CA-039*  
India’s BRICS presidency consolidated the grouping as a more diverse, strategically autonomous Global South forum rather than a China-led or explicitly anti-Western bloc, while continuing to treat Russia and Iran as legitimate international actors.[6][8] The GCC reinforced its collective economic weight—roughly $5 trillion in sovereign-wealth assets and $1.6 trillion in merchandise trade—alongside a stronger emphasis on manufacturing, technology, AI, clean energy, logistics, and infrastructure.[2] Gulf capital is also turning more domestic: Qatar created a QIA division for domestic investment, reflecting a broader shift toward resilience, industrial policy, and supply-chain security rather than unrestricted overseas deployment.[10][3] Overall, the week showed meaningful momentum in parallel-track hedging: BRICS institutional positioning on the diplomatic side and increasingly coordinated Gulf financial and economic autonomy on the capital side.
